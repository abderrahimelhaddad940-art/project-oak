# Product Marketing Context

**Document version:** v2
**Last updated:** 2026-08-05

## Product Overview
**One-liner:** A DIY estate planning document kit — 57 professionally drafted, plain-English legal templates so any family can finish a complete, valid estate plan themselves, without an attorney's price tag or timeline.

**What it does:** GuardTies sells a flat-price digital download containing 57 estate planning documents (revocable living trust, last will & testament, financial and medical powers of attorney, healthcare directive, HIPAA authorization, trust funding checklist, asset inventories, guardian nomination, and advanced protection/legacy documents), organized into 8 guided sections. A 5-minute needs-assessment questionnaire tells buyers which documents apply to their situation before they open a single one. Every document includes plain-English guidance notes, and a 50-state notary & witness guide covers legal execution. Delivered as editable Word (.docx) + fillable PDF within 24 hours of purchase.

**Product category:** DIY / self-serve estate planning document kits — adjacent to (but distinct from) online will-maker platforms.

**Product type:** Digital info product — a one-time document bundle download, not SaaS. No account, no login, no dashboard.

**Business model:** Flat one-time fee — $499 Individual / $599 Couple. No subscription, no recurring billing, no upsell tiers. Sold via direct checkout (site currently has 2 checkout pages being wired in).

## Target Audience
**Target customers:** Individual consumers and households (B2C) — ordinary families, not high-net-worth individuals who already have an attorney on retainer.

**Decision-makers:** The individual or couple themselves. Self-service purchase, no buying committee.

**Primary use case:** Getting a complete, legally valid estate plan *finished* — without attorney fees, and without getting stuck on a blank template.

**Jobs to be done:**
- Protect my family / minor children if something happens to me (guardianship, minor's trust)
- Keep my family out of probate court (privacy + speed + lower cost)
- Finally finish the estate plan I've been putting off

**Use cases / segments** (the site's "Who it's for" personas):
- The New Parent — guardianship & minor's trust

- The Blended Family — specific allocations & spendthrift clauses
- The Procrastinator — fast setup & peace of mind

- The Wealth Builder — privacy & multi-generational transfer

Also positioned as a fit for: married couples, single adults, retirees, business owners, unmarried partners, pet owners.

## Problems & Pain Points
**Core problem:** People know they need an estate plan but every visible option feels wrong — an attorney means a $3,500–$10,000+ quote and weeks of back-and-forth; a free template online means a blank document with no idea what applies or whether it's done right. So most people do nothing.

**Why alternatives fall short:**
- Attorneys: expensive, billed hourly, slow (weeks/months), requires appointments
- Free/blank DIY template bundles: no guidance on what applies to you, no state-specific direction, no execution instructions — easy to start, easy to abandon

**What it costs them:** Family exposed to probate — a public, 9–18 month court process that costs thousands in fees and puts financial affairs on public record; decisions about children/assets left to courts or state default instead of the family; emotional and administrative burden placed on grieving family members at the worst possible time.

**Emotional tension:** Guilt over procrastination ("I keep putting it off"), quiet anxiety about doing something this important wrong, fear of losing control over what happens to kids/assets, embarrassment that something "every responsible adult should have" still isn't done.

## Competitive Landscape
**Direct:** Generic DIY estate-planning template bundles (marketplace/Etsy-style document packs) — same solution (templates), same problem, but typically ship as blank documents with no sequencing, no plain-English guidance, and no funding/execution instructions, so buyers stall out after purchase.

**Secondary:** Online will-maker platforms (e.g. Trust & Will, LegalZoom, Rocket Lawyer, Nolo) — solve the same underlying problem through a guided web app rather than a document kit, often with subscription pricing, per-document fees, or upsell-heavy flows rather than one flat one-time price and permanent ownership.

**Indirect:** Estate planning attorneys / local law firms — the "proper" alternative, fully custom and attorney-reviewed, but slow and expensive. The single biggest indirect competitor is arguably inertia — doing nothing at all.

## Differentiation
**Key differentiators:**
- Needs-assessment questionnaire (5 min) before any document is opened — tells buyers exactly which of the 57 apply
- Plain-English guidance notes embedded in every document, not just legal boilerplate
- Trust funding checklist + 50-state notary & witness guide — covers the execution step most DIY kits skip entirely (a trust that isn't funded is "just paper")
- One flat price, no subscription — documents owned permanently
- 15 advanced protection documents (bypass/disclaimer trusts, step-up in basis, Medicaid planning) most DIY kits don't offer at any price

**How we do it differently:** GuardTies sells a *guided system*, not a document dump — sequencing, plain-English explanation, and execution guidance are bundled with the legal templates themselves.

**Why that's better:** It removes the two failure modes that sink most DIY estate plans even when the documents exist: overwhelm/no idea where to start, and documents that get signed but never properly funded/executed.

**Why customers choose GuardTies:** An attorney-equivalent, properly structured estate plan for a fraction of attorney cost, finished in a weekend, with no appointments, no hourly billing, and a 30-day money-back guarantee.

## Objections
| Objection | Response |
|-----------|----------|
| "Is $499 worth it compared to an attorney?" | Attorney-equivalent runs $3,500–$10,000+; $499/$599 covers 57 documents (vs. a handful an attorney would itemize at that price), backed by a 30-day money-back guarantee that removes the risk. |
| "I'm not a lawyer — can I actually do this correctly?" | Every document has plain-English guidance notes; the needs assessment sequences the work; the 50-state notary & witness guide gives exact execution steps for your state. |
| "Does this replace an attorney entirely / is a DIY trust legally valid?" | Yes, for the large majority of families — same structural clauses and execution requirements as an attorney-drafted plan. For genuinely complex situations (contested guardianship, multi-state business succession, estates near the federal estate tax exemption), it's the foundation — bring in a specialist only for that piece. |

**Anti-persona:** High-net-worth individuals or complex, contested, or multi-entity estates (business succession disputes, estates near/over the federal estate tax exemption) who need bespoke attorney-drafted structures and ongoing counsel. Also not a fit: people who want an ongoing software/dashboard experience — GuardTies is a static document download, not a SaaS product with accounts or updates pushed to you.

## Switching Dynamics
**Push:** A life event forcing the issue (new baby, home purchase, health scare, aging parent, remarriage/blended family); growing guilt at being "the one who never got around to it"; watching a friend or relative go through a messy, expensive probate process.

**Pull:** A concrete price anchor ($499/$599 vs. $3,500–$10,000+); the 24-hour delivery promise; explicit reassurance that guidance is included ("no legal knowledge required"); the 30-day guarantee removing purchase risk.

**Habit:** "I'll get to it eventually" inertia; not knowing where to start; having already downloaded a free template once and stalled out (this is literally the "Almost-Done" persona).

**Anxiety:** "What if I do this wrong and it's not legally binding?" "What if my state has different rules?" "What if this isn't actually as good as an attorney?" "What if my situation changes later?" — all directly answered in the FAQ, which is a good signal these are the real top-of-mind objections.

|------|---------|
