Common myths

5 beliefs that are keeping your family
unprotected right now.

These aren't edge cases. They are the exact thoughts that have delayed estate planning for millions of Americans — including, probably, you. Each one is false. Here's the truth.

1
"I'm not wealthy enough to need a trust."
A trust isn't about wealth — it's about control and privacy. If you own a home, have children, have a bank account, or simply want to decide who gets your belongings, a trust protects you. The people who need trusts least are the very wealthy, who have attorneys on retainer. The people who need them most are ordinary families — who have nobody looking out for them.
2
"A will is enough."
A will goes through probate — the public court process that takes 9–18 months, costs thousands in fees, and makes your financial affairs a matter of public record. A revocable living trust bypasses probate entirely. Assets transfer directly to your beneficiaries, privately, within days. A will and a trust serve different purposes; a complete estate plan includes both.
3
"Once I sign it, I'm locked in forever."
A revocable living trust — which is what GuardTies provides — can be changed, updated, or revoked at any time while you're alive. Had another child? Got divorced? Bought a new property? You update it. That's what the trust amendment form is for. You are never locked in.
4
"A trust eliminates estate taxes."
A revocable living trust does not reduce estate taxes on its own — your assets are still part of your taxable estate. What it does do is transfer them privately and quickly, without probate. If tax minimization is a priority, the advanced protection documents in GuardTies — bypass trust structures, disclaimer trust provisions, step-up in basis clauses — address these strategies specifically.
5
"I'll sort this out when I'm older."
Estate plans can only be created while you have legal capacity. If you lose that capacity — through accident, illness, or sudden incapacity — it becomes legally impossible to create one. The people who most need a trust are those who think they have more time. You don't know when the window closes. You only know it will.

"What's the difference between a trust and a will — and do I need both?"
+

A will tells people what you want after you die — but it goes through probate court, which is public, slow (9–18 months), and expensive. A revocable living trust does the same job but bypasses probate entirely: assets transfer directly to your beneficiaries, privately, within days.

The short answer: you need both. A pour-over will catches anything you didn't transfer into your trust during your lifetime. Your trust handles the rest. GuardTies includes both, along with the healthcare and financial documents that complete the picture.

Bottom line: A will is your backstop. A trust is your primary vehicle. Together they cover everything.
"Do I actually need a trust, or is a will enough?"
+

If any of the following is true, a trust is worth it: you own real estate, you have children under 18, you have assets you want distributed in a specific way, or you value keeping your estate private. A trust bypasses probate — the public court process that ties up your estate and costs your family time and money at the worst possible moment.

If your estate is extremely simple — no real estate, no children, minimal assets — a will may be sufficient. But the cost of creating a trust that turns out to be unnecessary is small. The cost of not having one when your family needs it is not.

"Is a revocable trust flexible? Can I change it later?"
+

Yes — completely. A revocable living trust can be changed, updated, or revoked at any time while you are alive and have legal capacity. Got divorced? Had another child? Bought a new property? Changed your mind about a beneficiary? You update it using the trust amendment form included in your package.

An irrevocable trust is different — once created, it generally cannot be changed. GuardTies provides revocable trusts, which is the right choice for the overwhelming majority of families. You are never locked in.
